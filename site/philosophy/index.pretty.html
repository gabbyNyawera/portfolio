How I think

Design should feel alive. These are the principles I return to, and the process I use to keep them honest.

See it in the work

“Design should feel alive.”

Not loud. Not decorated. Alive: responsive, clear, and built around how people actually behave.

Clarity

“If it needs explanation, it’s not done.”

Cleverness is easy. Clarity is hard. The best design is the design you don’t notice — it just works. If someone has to think too much, I’ve failed.

Functionality

“Form follows function — not the other way around.”

Every element should earn its place by solving a problem, not by looking interesting. If it doesn’t serve a purpose, it doesn’t stay.

Whitespace

“What you leave out defines what remains.”

Breathing room. The space between elements is just as important as the elements themselves. Restraint isn’t minimalism — it’s focus.

Intentionality

“Nothing is accidental. Every pixel earns its place.”

Design for human error. Every interaction, every word should be there for a reason. If you can’t explain why it exists, it probably shouldn’t.

Process

Five steps, repeated until the work is clear.

01 — Discover

Discover

Understand before creating.

Read the problem before sketching the solution — interviews, audits, and a clear brief in writing.

02 — Define

Define

Clarify the problem.

Turn findings into a brief the whole team can hold — constraints agreed before anything is made.

03 — Design

Design

Explore structure.

Structure first, surface last — wireframes and prototypes tested against real content and people.

04 — Deliver

Deliver

Refine until ready.

Build the details, document the system, and hand it over so clean it asks no questions.

05 — Iterate

Iterate

Remove until it breaks.

Watch it in use, cut what isn’t working, and repeat — the first version is never the last.

I design experiences
that feel alive.

Thoughtful interfaces, clear systems, and experiences built around how people actually behave.
