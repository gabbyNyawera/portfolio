Display · the loud option

Loud, but
still considered.

Attention is not the same as volume. A display face earns the front of a page by doing one thing the reader has not seen before — and then getting out of the way for everything else.

These five are louder than the didones on the other sheets: Melodrama and Boska from Fontshare, Zodiak and Panchang alongside them, Gloock from Google. Four of them break a convention on purpose; one of them is simply expensive-looking.

Each is set at the same size on the same sentence, so the difference is the letterform and nothing else.

01

Scale does the work

The fastest way to attention is size. A face only has to survive 6rem with its hairlines intact — everything below that is a different decision.

02

Contrast reads as cost

Thick stem to hairline is the oldest trick in display type. It makes a page look lit from one side, and it signals that someone chose it.

03

One wrong detail

Every memorable face has a feature that should not work: a flagged t, a flat-topped a, a lowercase that is not quite the width you expect.

Distinct

Overscale and crop

Set the word larger than its container and let the box cut it. The reader completes it. Cheapest drama available, and it still looks intentional.

calm considered distinctive

One family, three pressures

Melodrama ships as static cuts, not a variable font — 400, 500 and 700 at about 22kb each. Three small files still buy you a headline, a subhead and a shout.

Calm, quietly distinctive.

Emphasis without italic

Melodrama has no italic. Swap the emphasis to colour, or to a second face, and the sentence still bends — often more sharply than a slant would manage.

The call

Melodrama for the front, Boska if you want the italic

If the page should stop someone scrolling, Melodrama is the answer and the missing italic is a feature: emphasis moves to colour, which suits a site that already has an accent colour to spend. If you would rather keep the italic-in-the-headline pattern, Boska does the same job with more restraint and a full weight range.

Licensing, honestly

Melodrama, Boska, Zodiak and Panchang come from Fontshare, which is free for personal and commercial use and allows self-hosting — but under the ITF Free Font Licence, not the SIL Open Font Licence the rest of this site uses. That is a different kind of permission, so read it before shipping rather than taking my word for it.

Gloock is plain OFL and drops into the existing pipeline with no new terms at all.

If you adopt Melodrama

One token changes: --font-display. Keep Newsreader and Figtree where they are, set the display face at 2rem and up, and put the emphasis on --accent-ink instead of an italic. That single substitution is what carries the whole look.

Then the existing rules still apply: display never sets body copy, one loud word per screen, and the mark follows whatever the display face becomes.
